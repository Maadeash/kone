## 4. Did the envelope features actually help?

| Model | raw | envelope only | raw+envelope | gain vs raw |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MLP | 0.5657 | 0.5714 | 0.6025 | +0.0368 |
| RF | 0.5830 | 0.5819 | 0.5954 | +0.0124 |

### Verdict

- **MLP**: gain of +0.0368 is smaller than the fold-to-fold std (0.1342) -- NOT distinguishable from fold noise. Do not claim an improvement from this.
  - Envelope features alone (0.5714) beat the original 104 (0.5657), which suggests the physics features carry complementary information even if the concatenation does not exploit it.
- **RF**: gain of +0.0124 is smaller than the fold-to-fold std (0.1227) -- NOT distinguishable from fold noise. Do not claim an improvement from this.
  - Envelope features alone score 0.5819 vs 0.5830 for raw, so on their own they are weaker than the general-purpose statistics.

### Honest reading of the 80% target

Best 4-class leave-bearing-out accuracy across all feature sets and models in this run: **0.6025**.

If that number is well below 0.80, say so in the paper rather than reaching for a split that inflates it. Published leave-one-bearing-out results on this dataset are far below the same-bearing numbers that dominate the literature, and Lessmeier et al. themselves report a large domain shift between artificially damaged and naturally degraded bearings. A well-characterised 60-70% under a genuinely unseen-bearing protocol, with the ceiling explained, is a stronger contribution than an unverifiable 98% from a leaky split -- and the leaky split is exactly the failure mode this project already diagnosed and rejected.

## 8. Assumptions carried into these numbers

- **Bearing internal geometry is ASSUMED** (6203: Z=8, Bd=6.747 mm, Pd=28.5 mm, alpha=0). The KAt documentation gives the designation only, not the manufacturer or internal geometry. Run `python envelope_features.py --show-frequencies` for the sensitivity figures.

- **Pole pair number p=4 is CONFIRMED** (Lessmeier et al., PHME 2016). The drive is inverter-fed, so there is no fixed 50/60 Hz line frequency: f_elec = 4 x rpm/60, i.e. 100 Hz at 1500 rpm and 60 Hz at 900 rpm. Verify on your data with `python envelope_features.py --diagnose`.

- **The vibration demodulation band (1-10 kHz) is ASSUMED.** A kurtogram-selected band per recording would likely be better but is not fixed-function-friendly for the FPGA pipeline.

- **Envelope spectrum resolution is 4 Hz** at the current 0.25 s window. FTF sits near 9.5 Hz at 1500 rpm, i.e. barely two bins from DC. If the FTF and low-harmonic features look uninformative here, that is a resolution artefact and work item 5 (longer windows) is the correct follow-up, not deletion of the feature.
